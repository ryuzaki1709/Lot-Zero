# Lot Zero — Product, System, and Experience Design

**Status:** Proposed implementation specification  
**Date:** 2026-08-14  
**Hackathon:** All Things Agentic Hackathon — Taskmaster track  
**Selected visual target:** Latest ideation set, Option 3 — Incident War Room  

## 1. Product Thesis

Lot Zero is an evidence-backed recall-scope and containment workspace for food manufacturers. It converts an incoming safety signal into a durable, auditable incident run: extracting cited facts, reconciling trace records, executing only pre-authorized reversible actions, routing consequential decisions to accountable humans, monitoring acknowledgements, and refusing closure while evidence remains incomplete.

The product is not a chatbot, autonomous regulatory authority, legal-compliance oracle, or generic multi-agent dashboard. Its defining behavior is disciplined agency: act quickly where policy permits, stop where authority or evidence ends, and preserve proof of both.

## 2. Judge-Facing Promise

Within ten seconds, the primary screen must answer:

1. What event woke the agent?
2. What has the agent actually done?
3. Which work came from Gemini, deterministic code, or a human decision?
4. Why is the run blocked, retrying, or awaiting input?
5. What persisted state changed in Google Cloud?

The uninterrupted 90-second product sequence inside the four-minute submission is:

`fresh signal → cited scope → deterministic impact → provisional hold → human authorization → retry/recovery → acknowledgements → honestly blocked closure`

## 3. Intended Users and Authority

### Primary user

The Recall Coordinator owns the case timeline, validates public scope, coordinates role handoffs, reviews action receipts, escalates missing acknowledgements, and requests closure review.

### Supporting roles

- **QA:** validates affected scope and labeling/science claims; authorizes containment policy where required.
- **Regulatory Affairs:** approves regulator-facing or public communication when the scenario requires it; this role is outside the first live path.
- **Customer Operations:** reviews recipient sets, sends approved demo-sink communications, and monitors synthetic acknowledgements.

### Non-negotiable authority boundaries

Lot Zero may autonomously:

- ingest and hash a notice or event;
- extract a structured scope with citations;
- validate schema and deterministic predicates;
- reconcile aliases and propose candidate matches;
- open a provisional case and assign internal tasks;
- apply a provisional soft hold only when standing policy `EVAL-HOLD-01` matches the target type, tenant, maximum quantity, reversibility requirement, and 30-minute expiry;
- draft communications;
- retry safe idempotent operations;
- schedule effectiveness checks and escalations.

Lot Zero may not autonomously:

- declare an official or legal recall;
- assign regulatory severity;
- permanently quarantine, release, destroy, or return inventory;
- contact customers or regulators;
- authorize compensation;
- remove a hold;
- close or reopen a case.

Scope validation, continuation/expansion of containment, recipient/payload approval, external communication, and closure are separate decisions. Approval of one never silently grants another. The agent service account and the person who requested an override cannot approve that override.

## 4. Data Authenticity Contract

The demo separates three provenance classes everywhere—in data, UI, logs, README, and video:

1. **Evaluation signal facts:** facts from a version-controlled, fictional lab/quality event linked to exact passages or fields.
2. **Evaluation-scenario records:** manually authored, internally consistent fixtures used to exercise ERP/WMS/order/recipient behavior for fictional tenant `EVAL-TENANT-01`.
3. **Runtime evidence:** real IDs, timestamps, model calls, Firestore writes, Pub/Sub deliveries, Cloud Run revision metadata, traces, retries, and action receipts generated by the running deployment.

The live hero scenario begins on August 14, 2026 with a fully fictional internal lab event for neutral identifiers `EVAL-TENANT-01`, ingredient lot `ING-4417`, and finished product `FP-100`. The fixture and golden expected outcomes are published in the repository. No generated brand, real company, real recipient, or inferred private record appears in this live path.

An authoritative public FDA announcement is retained only as a separate document-extraction regression fixture. It is not the live containment trigger and is never joined to fictional private records.

The environment header persistently but quietly says `Evaluation tenant · synthetic records · no real outreach`. Demo-sink callbacks are labeled synthetic; runtime infrastructure receipts are labeled real.

## 5. Core Records

- `RecallCase`: case ID, owner, status, scope version, environment, deadlines.
- `SourceNotice`: content-addressed object generation, SHA-256 hash, issuer, received/published timestamps, and verifiable page/character spans.
- `AffectedScope`: versioned typed claims, evidence spans, `verified | needs_review | unresolved` state, and overrides.
- `ProductMaster`: scenario product identifiers and aliases.
- `LotGenealogy`: scenario ingredient, transformation, finished-lot, and parent/child edges.
- `InventoryPosition`: scenario site, bin, lot, quantity, availability, hold state, reconciliation timestamp.
- `Shipment` / `Order`: scenario allocations, destinations, timestamps, recipient references.
- `ContainmentAction`: requested action, target, standing-policy ID, expiry, reversibility, domain action key, provider idempotency token, state, result, and compensation/reconciliation state.
- `Approval`: authenticated principal, role, tenant/case scope, policy/scope/payload versions, decision, rationale, and timestamp.
- `NotificationPacket`: versioned audience and payload hashes, approval state, provider state, and retry state. A content change creates a new version and invalidates prior approval.
- `Acknowledgement`: recipient, channel, receipt, timestamp, verification state.
- `EvidenceLedger`: actor-attributed, hash-linked source span, structured value, validator, tool call, override, transition, and prior-entry hash. It is append-only by service policy, not intrinsically immutable.
- `EventOutbox`: domain event ID, Pub/Sub delivery/message ID, correlation ID, event version, attempts, acknowledgement, and dead-letter status.

No UI claim may exist without a backing record or an explicit `not available` state.

## 6. State Machine

Primary path for the first build:

`SIGNAL_RECEIVED → SCOPE_REVIEW → PROVISIONAL_CONTAINMENT → ACTION_REVIEW → ACK_MONITORING → EFFECTIVENESS_CHECK → CLOSED`

Orthogonal recovery status:

- `NEEDS_INFORMATION`
- `FAILED_RETRYABLE`
- `BLOCKED`

Each recovery status retains its parent phase and explicit return phase. `NEEDS_INFORMATION` returns after authorized correction; `FAILED_RETRYABLE` returns after successful bounded retry or moves to `BLOCKED`; `BLOCKED` returns only after the named evidence or approval arrives. Scope versions are append-only by service policy. An expansion creates a new version and delta plan. Compare-and-set preconditions reject stale events and approvals. Closure requires the named evaluation closure policy, resolved blockers, effectiveness evidence, and authorized human approval.

## 7. Architecture

### Google Cloud execution path

- **Cloud Storage:** versioned source objects with generation pinning and content hashes; no immutability claim without a configured retention lock.
- **Pub/Sub:** `incident-signals` topic → authenticated push subscription → Cloud Run `/events`; bounded retry and `incident-signals-dlq`. DLQ replay preserves the domain event ID while receiving a new Pub/Sub message ID.
- **Cloud Run:** one ADK orchestration service plus deterministic in-process or separately deployed tool endpoints as justified by the implementation plan.
- **Google ADK:** one orchestrator workflow, not a theatrical swarm. The exact released package/version is pinned in the lockfile and shown from runtime metadata.
- **Gemini:** cited extraction, constrained reconciliation, explanation, and drafting using the exact contest-compliant model identifier pinned in configuration and returned by runtime—not a hard-coded UI label.
- **Firestore:** case state, checkpoints, scope versions, approvals, action ledger, outbox, acknowledgement state.
- **Cloud Logging and Cloud Trace:** correlation across trigger, model call, validation, tools, state writes, and recovery.
- **Secret Manager and IAM:** distinct least-privilege service accounts, authenticated Cloud Run ingress, tenant-scoped server-side authorization, secret isolation, audit logging, and denial of cross-tenant access.

### Reasoning and execution split

- Gemini receives bounded source text and handles ambiguous document interpretation, candidate reconciliation, and language generation. Structured output includes source hash and exact page/character offsets. Source content is data and cannot alter policies or tool authority.
- Deterministic code handles schema validation, lot/date predicates, graph traversal, quantities, permissions, state transitions, idempotency, deadlines, and side effects.
- Humans handle consequential authority.

The UI exposes structured decisions, evidence, validation rules, tool inputs/outputs, and concise rationale. It never exposes or claims to expose hidden chain-of-thought.

Recipient PII is excluded from Gemini prompts. Logs and traces contain IDs, hashes, or redacted summaries rather than raw recipient data or full notices.

### Delivery semantics

Pub/Sub delivery is treated as at-least-once. Duplicate suppression uses a stable domain event/action key, never the Pub/Sub message ID. A Firestore transaction reserves the action and its outbox entry with `PLANNED → IN_FLIGHT → SUCCEEDED | FAILED | UNKNOWN` states. Each adapter receives an idempotency token. A crash after an ambiguous external response enters reconciliation rather than blind retry. The supported claim is **safe retry with one externally visible effect**, proven by adapter idempotency plus reconciliation—not exactly-once execution.

Checkpoint/resume is Lot Zero application logic persisted in Firestore and orchestrated by ADK; it is not represented as an ADK or Firestore exactly-once guarantee. Approved communication content is reused by hash across retries. A changed scope, recipient set, or payload creates a new version and invalidates the prior approval.

## 8. Failure and Recovery Contract

The main video proves one recovery path: the demo notification sink injects a retryable failure, Lot Zero persists the checkpoint, retries the identical approved payload, and records a single externally visible sink receipt. Duplicate suppression appears in the evidence/test segment. The wider evaluation suite covers:

- duplicate event delivery → suppress duplicate actions;
- stale or out-of-order scope update → reject older version;
- ambiguous lot notation → enter `NEEDS_INFORMATION`;
- prompt injection inside source data → preserve as data and deny policy mutation;
- malformed Gemini output → schema rejection and bounded retry;
- model or tool quota failure → exponential backoff then dead letter;
- partial inventory update → reconcile against action ledger;
- worker crash → checkpoint resume with adapter idempotency and ambiguous-result reconciliation;
- stale inventory snapshot → require warehouse confirmation;
- scope expansion → execute only the delta;
- demo-sink provider failure → retry the identical approved payload;
- missing acknowledgement → escalate and keep closure locked.

The submission must show one actual retry/recovery and one actual duplicate suppression backed by persisted receipts, not animations disconnected from state.

## 9. Selected Visual System

### Foundation

The selected visual target is the third image from the latest ideation set: **Incident War Room**. The reference image is 1487×1058. Its core motif is a living actor-attributed, hash-linked chronology across accountable role lanes.

### Visual language

- Warm ivory base rather than a cool generic admin canvas.
- Deep navy structure and primary controls.
- Oxblood for risk or blocked state.
- Amber for human attention and retryable uncertainty.
- Forest for verified receipts.
- Status is always expressed with icon, text, and position—not color alone.
- IBM Plex Sans or equivalent precise sans for controls and operational data.
- Source Serif 4 or equivalent readable serif for source titles and formal decisions.
- Body size 14–16px at the 1440×1024 implementation viewport. A QA crop of the 1487×1058 reference is approved before pixel comparison.
- Hairline rules and alignment establish grouping; rounded containers and shadows are rare.
- No gradients, glass, neon, chatbot bubbles, agent avatars, KPI wall, generic sidebar, decorative charts, stock imagery, emoji, or fake “thinking” animation.

### Primary screen: Incident War Room

1. **Priority header** — case, current state, elapsed time, and `Evaluation tenant · synthetic records · no real outreach`. Runtime model, run, revision, message, correlation, and trace metadata move into a proof drawer.
2. **Role lanes** — Lot Zero automation, Recall Coordinator, QA, and Customer Operations. Regulatory Affairs is excluded from the first live path and remains a distinct role when a later scenario needs it.
3. **Attributed event packets** — typed events crossing lanes with timestamp/timezone, authenticated actor, role, policy, scope version, evidence count, input/output hash, and side-effect receipt. Handwritten signatures are not used.
4. **Recovery path** — adapter failure, persisted checkpoint, resume, approved-payload reuse, and duplicate prevention shown in chronology.
5. **Incident truth rail** — before/after Firestore state deltas for affected scenario inventory/orders, approved actions, outstanding acknowledgements, and exact versioned evaluation closure criterion.
6. **Event spine** — live run from signal through blocked closure; read-only historical inspection is a clearly different state and never reverses side effects.
7. **Current action** — create a sandbox-only internal escalation task for the outstanding synthetic acknowledgement; supporting action opens the recovery receipt.

### Layout measurements

- 1440×1024 primary viewport; 56px priority header; 112px scenario/source summary; 92px event spine.
- 286px incident-truth rail; remaining width belongs to the chronology.
- Within the chronology, a 148px sticky lane-label column precedes the event canvas.
- Event packets use 176–208px readable widths, 12px internal spacing, and no body copy below 14px.
- Technical metadata is progressively disclosed in a 400px evidence drawer rather than shrinking the main chronology.
- At 200% zoom or below 760px CSS width, the spatial lanes become a single semantic chronological list; simultaneous two-axis scrolling is forbidden.

### Supporting modes

- **Evidence Ledger:** source passages linked to typed claims and validation rules.
- **Traceability Evidence:** an expandable affected-lot graph inside the selected trace packet, including one broken or ambiguous edge.
- **Approval Dossier:** authoritative decision view with role, rationale, permitted effects, and actions that remain locked.
- **Recovery Receipt:** checkpoint, attempts, payload hash, idempotency key, provider response, and final result.

These are complementary modes, not alternate visual skins. The War Room is the judge-facing hero; the others open from packets without changing the underlying case.

## 10. Required Corrections to the Generated Target

The generated image is a composition reference, not a source of truth. Implementation must correct:

- `Gemini 2.5 Pro` → the exact contest-compliant Gemini runtime model ID verified from the final rules, deployed configuration, and response metadata.
- Generated people, signatures, recipients, domains, counts, IDs, and timestamps → deterministic records from the version-controlled evaluation dataset.
- Historical real-company source/operations pairing → a fully fictional current evaluation signal and tenant in the live path; the public FDA notice is a separate extraction regression fixture.
- `Scope review in progress` while later actions appear complete → state derived from the actual live run position.
- Any approval sequence that could imply QA classified a provider failure → failures are machine-observed; QA approves scope/containment, not technical diagnosis.
- Combined `Regulatory Affairs / Customer Operations` lane → distinct authority; only Customer Operations appears in the first live path.
- `Signed` packets and handwritten signatures → actor-attributed, hash-linked records with authenticated principal, role, rationale, approval ID, and timestamp.
- Any ornamental run metadata → values must come from the running service or visibly say unavailable.

## 11. Interaction Contract

- `Reset evaluation tenant` seeds the published fixture. `Start incident` publishes a real Pub/Sub event and begins a real backend run. It is the hero path.
- Play/pause controls affect visualization only; they never pause backend processing. Historical inspection selects persisted events but never reverses side effects.
- Selecting an event packet opens its evidence drawer without navigating away.
- Retry and checkpoint packets open a recovery receipt.
- Acknowledgement receipts filter the incident truth rail.
- `Create sandbox escalation task` creates an internal scenario task/event and updates the ledger; it never contacts a real recipient.
- Consequential actions require an authenticated principal, server-side tenant/case-scoped RBAC, correct authority, separation of duties, rationale, and compare-and-set against case/scope/payload/policy versions.
- Approval review exposes exact scope version, recipient/payload hash, permitted effects, still-locked actions, rationale, confirmation, rejection, and stale-version failure.
- Locked actions explain both why they are locked and which evidence/approval unlocks them.
- Focus order follows skip link → start/current run controls → chronological packets → current safe action → incident truth → supporting evidence.
- Drawers move focus inside, support Escape, return focus to the originating packet, and trap focus only when modal.
- All controls have visible keyboard focus, descriptive labels, and a minimum 44×44 CSS-pixel target. Text contrast is at least 4.5:1 and control/state contrast at least 3:1.
- No replay autostarts. A restrained live region announces the current step, retry, failure, and blocked state. Reduced motion preserves all information.
- The DOM contains a semantic chronological list associating every packet with sequence, lane, actor, status, and predecessor; arrows and position are supplemental.
- Packet states are defined for default, hover, selected, focus-visible, running, retrying, blocked, stale, failed, and completed. Every state uses icon plus text inside the packet.
- Loading, empty, unavailable, permission-denied, failed-receipt, and disconnected-trace states use backed status records and actionable recovery copy.
- IDs and hashes are visually truncated with a labeled copy action for the full runtime-derived value. Timestamps show local timezone and expose UTC in details.

## 12. Demo Scenario

The repository includes a manually authored fictional evaluation tenant and human-reviewable golden expected outcomes. Gemini does not generate the fixture or ground truth. The evaluation workflow maps to a named internal recall SOP but does not claim certified regulatory compliance. FDA/FSMA terminology is used only where applicability has been verified; otherwise it is labeled as an internal trace model.

Demo sequence:

1. Reset the evaluation tenant.
2. Start the fresh fictional lab incident through the real Pub/Sub trigger.
3. Gemini resolves an ambiguous label/lot alias into cited candidates; deterministic schema and human controls validate the selected scope.
4. Deterministic genealogy identifies affected/unaffected records and visibly mutates eligible inventory/orders to `PROVISIONAL_HOLD` under standing policy `EVAL-HOLD-01`; the packet shows policy, target set, reversibility, and expiry.
5. QA authorizes continuation/expansion, then Customer Operations separately approves the versioned recipient set and demo-sink payload.
6. The demo sink injects one retryable failure; Lot Zero resumes from a Firestore checkpoint and reuses the identical approved payload without producing a duplicate sink receipt.
7. Synthetic acknowledgements arrive; one remains outstanding. Lot Zero creates an internal escalation task and refuses closure under versioned policy `EVAL-CLOSE-01`.

The video ends with the unresolved evidence visible rather than manufacturing a perfect closure.

### Four-minute submission structure

- 0:00–0:20 — problem, claim boundary, and evaluation label.
- 0:20–1:50 — uninterrupted live incident from Pub/Sub signal through provisional holds and approval.
- 1:50–2:35 — injected provider failure, checkpoint recovery, and identical-payload proof.
- 2:35–3:10 — ADK/Gemini/deterministic/human architecture split.
- 3:10–3:40 — golden metrics, before/after Firestore records, Cloud Run/Pub/Sub/Trace proof.
- 3:40–4:00 — unresolved acknowledgement, blocked closure, and concise conclusion.

## 13. Evaluation and Testing

### Golden scenario tests

At minimum:

- exact product/date/lot match;
- unaffected adjacent batch;
- shared ingredient across finished lots;
- missing downstream mapping;
- duplicate signal;
- stale scope event;
- worker restart;
- action retry;
- approval denied;
- late acknowledgement;
- attempted prompt injection;
- malformed model output;
- authorization violation;
- scope expansion delta;
- closure blocked by missing evidence.
- correct and forbidden tool selection;
- approval-version race and stale approval;
- concurrent Pub/Sub deliveries;
- ambiguous external timeout reconciliation;
- DLQ replay preserving the domain event ID;
- cross-tenant authorization denial;
- prompt-injection exfiltration denial.

### Metrics

- affected-record precision and recall against golden ground truth;
- false-hold rate;
- unresolved-record rate;
- duplicate actions prevented;
- recovery success after restart;
- acknowledgement coverage;
- citation span precision and recall;
- median and worst observed time to provisional containment across repeated runs, reported with scenario size and environment;
- recovery-time distribution and one-visible-effect invariant.

Metrics are computed by deterministic tests, never by the model under evaluation. Claims remain limited to the versioned evaluation scenario.

An ablation compares deterministic-only alias/lot reconciliation with Gemini-assisted candidate generation on the same manually authored golden set. A claim-evidence table links every submission claim to a test, persisted record, log, trace, or deployed artifact.

### Product verification

- unit and property tests for validators, graph traversal, state machine, and idempotency;
- integration tests with Pub/Sub and Firestore emulators;
- deployed smoke test on Cloud Run / Pub/Sub / Firestore;
- keyboard and focus testing;
- contrast and zoom/reflow checks;
- browser verification of the complete live incident and approval path;
- 200% zoom, 320-CSS-pixel stacked-list behavior, semantic chronology, keyboard, focus, and live-region verification;
- visual comparison against an approved 1440×1024 crop of the 1487×1058 selected target;
- design QA must pass before handoff.

## 14. Definition of Done

Lot Zero is ready for submission only when:

- the required Google model, ADK framework, and GCP infrastructure are genuinely used;
- the judge reset reproduces the scenario without hidden manual edits;
- real runtime metadata and state mutations are inspectable;
- evidence, policies, approvals, retries, and recovery are connected by correlation ID;
- before/after Firestore or API records prove every hold shown in the UI;
- the runtime proves ADK owns orchestration and tool execution, not merely an import;
- deployment evidence records project/region, Cloud Run service/revision, Pub/Sub delivery, Firestore mutation, exact Gemini endpoint, pinned ADK version, and matching architecture diagram;
- simulated adapters and scenario records are labeled everywhere;
- the main demo can be understood without narration;
- the submission explains why the product belongs in Taskmaster: it completes a bounded operational mission across time, tools, approvals, retries, and durable follow-up;
- the user can explain every architectural decision and run the project independently;
- deployed proof, README, architecture diagram, evaluation report, Devpost story, and demo video agree on what is real, simulated, and planned.
